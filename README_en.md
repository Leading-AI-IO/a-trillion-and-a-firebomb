## 📖 Overview

In the same week of April 2026, AI company valuations exceeded one trillion dollars, a Molotov cocktail was hurled at their CEO's home, and 1,000 people lost their jobs.

This was not coincidence. These events were born from the same structure.

This book is written to depict that asymmetry. It does not provide answers. It does not write prescriptions. **It only depicts structure.**

Drawing on primary data from Pew Research Center, Gallup, the Edelman Trust Barometer, the Ipsos Global AI Monitor, and Stanford HAI, this book makes visible the nonlinear social-emotional structure in which hope and fear toward AI are **accelerating simultaneously**. It traces the 50-point perception gap between AI experts and the general public, the evaporation of entry-level jobs, the trillion-dollar concentration of AI capital within a 50km radius of San Francisco, the "permanent underclass" thesis as Piketty's r>g reaches its extreme in the AI era, the structural parallels between the 1811 Luddite movement and 2026, the gap between technology speed and institutional speed, the coexistence of sincerity and hypocrisy in AI companies' self-awareness, and Japan's singular structure — low adoption, low perceived benefit, high grievance — across 9 chapters and an epilogue.

**This book does not write answers. But when structure becomes visible, something changes within the reader. What was unseen becomes seen. And what has been seen can no longer be unseen.**
